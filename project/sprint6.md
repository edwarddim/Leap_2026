
# Project Kickoff
## Sprint 5
| # | Original | Plain-language version |
|---|----------|------------------------|
| 1 | A demo of the UI against the real backend, not a mock. | **See it working for real:** A live demonstration of the app using the actual system behind the scenes, not a simulated stand-in. |
| 2 | The screen spec, and one place the spec and the build disagreed and what you did about it. | **The plan versus what got built:** What the team planned for the screens to look like and do, one place where the finished product ended up different, and how they resolved it. |
| 3 | What your customer instructor thought when they saw it, praise, confusion or otherwise, and what the usability test found. | **What real people thought:** How the instructor acting as their customer reacted, including anything they liked or found confusing, and what the team learned from watching people try the app. |
| 4 | How you're handling an order that's accepted but not yet resolved. | **When the outcome is still unknown:** What the app does when an order has been received but its final result isn't known yet, including what the user sees during the wait. |
| 5 | Your test coverage and what it does and doesn't catch. | **How they know it works:** How much of the product the team's checks cover, what kinds of problems those checks catch, and what could still slip through. |
| 6 | An updated backlog, and what you're carrying into the applied project week. | **What's next:** The team's updated to-do list, and the work they're taking into the applied project week. |

## Sprint 6

| # | Original | Plain-language version |
|---|----------|------------------------|
| 1 | A walkthrough of your auth flow and the identity decisions behind it. | **How people sign in:** A step-by-step look at how users log in and prove who they are, and why the team chose to handle identity the way they did. |

| 4 | What the UI review against the screen spec found. | **Checking the screens against the plan:** What the team found when they compared the finished screens with the original design plan. |


# Presentation Suggestions
 - ### Prioritize creating a great presentation that covers most information over a rushed presentation that covers all topics
 - ### When doing a demo, run the demo multiple times to make sure bugs do not happen
 - ### Create a real life usage scenario to help convey the feature better
 - ### The person doing the demo should NOT be the one running the demo
